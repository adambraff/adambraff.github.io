Takeaways

Outdoor and active is the only category everyone agrees on. All five respondents rate it 0.9 to 1.4 points above their own personal average, the sole category where that holds for every rater. Every “favorite thing” answer named an activity, not a meal, museum, or hotel: the Vidden hike, kayaking, biking, the train and boat legs, the sauna-to-fjord jump. The five highest-scoring stops on the whole trip are either physical activities or the meal right after one.

Gothenburg was a forced audible, and it still underperformed. Air France canceled the CDG-to-Oslo flight, an air traffic controllers’ strike was on, and the fix was flying into Sweden, spending the night, and training up to Oslo the next morning. Given that, 6.1 as the leg average isn’t a surprise. Both the hotel and that train are in the bottom five stops of the entire trip. A real contingency plan for the next flight cancellation beats booking whatever’s closest that day.

Two stops split the group for real, not because of a tough grader. The H&M/Cos emergency shopping run and the Bryggen gelato stop show genuine disagreement even after adjusting for who tends to rate everything low or high: two people rated the shopping detour a 9, two rated it a 2 or 3. That’s a taste split worth planning around.

One respondent rates about two points lower than everyone else, on everything. That is a scale difference, not a satisfaction problem. Their free-text answer was positive, naming the post-hike food and drinks at Fløyen as their favorite part of the trip.

Food value at nice-but-not-Michelin restaurants in Norway got called out directly. One respondent’s suggested fix for the next non-foodie-capital country: go more casual.
