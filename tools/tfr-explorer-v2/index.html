H3: Fertility Trap / Path Dependence

Theory

Countries with low fertility experience self-reinforcing dynamics: smaller youth cohorts → tighter labor markets → higher wages → lower birth rates. A "trap" emerges where low fertility becomes self-sustaining.

Methods & Data Sources

Data Sources

  • Fertility Data: World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI), UN World Population Prospects 2022
  • Labor Force: WDI, ILO STAT
  • Development Indicators: WDI, UNDP Human Development Report
  • Policy Data: OECD Social Expenditure Database (SOCX)
  • Uncertainty Index: Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU) Index by Baker, Bloom, Davis
  • Digital Adoption: WDI, ITU World Telecommunication Indicators
  • US Social Trends: General Social Survey (GSS), NORC at UChicago, 1972–2022

Panel Regression Specification

Model: Two-way fixed effects (entity + year) ordinary least squares (OLS) with clustered standard errors at country level.

Equation: TFRit = α + β₁X₁ᵢₜ + β₂X₂ᵢₜ + ... + γᵢ + δₜ + εᵢₜ

Where γᵢ are country fixed effects and δₜ are year fixed effects. Year fixed effects control for global shocks (e.g., financial crises, pandemics). Where variables are collinear with time trends (e.g., technology adoption), some models use entity-only fixed effects to preserve variation.

Sample: 180+ countries, 1960–2024 (panel unbalanced). Detailed country analysis limited to 60+ countries with complete historical data.

Standard Errors: Clustered at country level to account for within-country autocorrelation.

Hypothesis Tests

Each hypothesis is tested via a dedicated regression model focusing on the key variable of interest. Coefficients are interpreted as the marginal effect of a 1-unit increase in the independent variable on TFR, holding fixed effects and other covariates constant.

Significance Levels:

  • *** p < 0.01 (Highly Significant)
  • ** p < 0.05 (Moderately Significant)
  • * p < 0.10 (Weakly Significant)

Machine Learning Model

Algorithm: Random Forest regression with 500 trees, max depth 20.

Features: All hypothesis variables, lagged effects, interactions, and squared terms. 42 total features after feature engineering.

Validation: 5-fold cross-validation. Performance metric: R² (explained variance).

Feature Importance: Mean Decrease Impurity (MDI) and permutation importance, showing which variables contribute most to predictions.

Limitations & Caveats

  • Observational Data: Correlations do not imply causation. Unmeasured confounders (cultural preferences, institutional factors) may bias results.
  • Reverse Causality: Some relationships may be bidirectional (e.g., HDI and fertility).
  • Missing Data: Panel is unbalanced; historical data sparse for developing countries.
  • Measurement Error: Fertility estimates in some countries rely on models, not direct surveys.
  • Time-Varying Confounders: Fixed effects address time-invariant omitted variables, but time-varying confounders remain a concern.

Reproducibility

All code, data, and results are available in the GitHub repository. Data files are cleaned, merged, and documented. Regression output includes full coefficient tables, diagnostics, and robustness checks.
