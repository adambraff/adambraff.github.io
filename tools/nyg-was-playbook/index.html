How to read a personnel number

When a substitution happens, count the offense's running backs and tight ends. The first digit is running backs (fullbacks included), the second is tight ends. Wide receivers are whatever is left of the five skill players. "12 personnel" means 1 back, 2 tight ends, 2 receivers. The defense matches with its own package, so a personnel change is the first clue to what's coming.

Four games of 2026 data is a small sample. Where this season's number isn't available, I note the coach's track record instead.

Most likely script, quarter by quarter

Q1

Washington tests the brace

Expect Blough to ease Daniels in with quick throws and the run game rather than designed QB runs. The rushing prop (28.5 yards, versus 50 a game before the injury) says the market expects the same. Watch whether Daniels keeps on any zone reads.

Q2

Giants go heavy

The Giants use two-back, two-tight-end sets on about a quarter of their snaps. That pulls Washington out of nickel and into its base defense, and the Giants try to grind the clock. They rank second in time of possession.

Q3

The thin secondary

Washington is down to Quan Martin and Jeremy Reaves at safety, and it's tied for last in second-half points allowed (17.8 a game). Second-half explosive plays are the Giants' best route to a win.

Q4

One-score finish

All three Giants wins came by one score, two of them sealed by late interceptions. They have outscored opponents by only 3 points total. If Daniels looks rusty, expect another tight fourth quarter decided by a turnover.
