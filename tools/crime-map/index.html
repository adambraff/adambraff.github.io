How the ranking works

Each case gets a severity from 1 to 10 by offense type (below), then a proximity factor of 1 / (1 + (distance ÷ h)²), where h is set by the slider (default 0.35 mi, so an incident 0.35 mi away counts half as much as one next door). Score = severity × proximity. Administrative entries such as warrants, traffic violations, lost property and requests for assistance score zero and are left out of the ranking, as are assaults on health care workers at Rhode Island Hospital (593 Eddy St), which are almost always patients and staff. Other cases logged at the hospital are tagged, since victims are often brought there from somewhere else. For serious cases the popup has a "News that week" link (a Google News search for Providence headlines about that kind of crime from the day before to a week after; street names turned out to be a poor filter, and most incidents never make the news) and, near Brown, a link to Brown's public-safety crime alerts. When a case lists several offenses, the most serious one counts.

The city publishes locations only at block level ("300 Block HOPE ST") or as intersections. A "block" is a hundred-number range: "100 Block WATERMAN ST" means any address from 100 to 199, and on streets with big lots that range can run half a mile (100–199 Waterman runs from Thayer St to past Cooke St). The map draws each ranked block as a colored stretch of street, placed with Census TIGER address ranges, with the numbered pin at its halfway point. The distance shown for a block is the average distance from home over that whole stretch, and the popup gives the nearest and farthest points. Records with only a street name are placed at the street's midpoint if the street is shorter than 0.4 mi and otherwise skipped (counted as "unplaced"). Distances are straight-line.
