A short, contested history

Norway spent about 400 years as a Danish province and 91 as a junior partner to Sweden. It has been fully independent since 1905, which makes it younger than the Brooklyn Bridge. Almost everything you associate with Norwegian national identity was invented in the 19th century by people trying to prove it existed.

c. 9500 BC
The ice leaves, people arrive

Hunter-gatherers reach an ice-free coast within a few centuries of deglaciation, from two directions: up the Atlantic coast (the Fosna culture) and around the top from the northeast (Komsa). Ancient DNA shows Western and Eastern Hunter-Gatherer ancestry meeting here early. Scandinavia was a genetic collision zone from the start.

c. 4000 BC
Farming arrives, mostly in the south

Anatolian-descended farmers spread up through Denmark. Norway's geography means agriculture never fully displaces hunting and fishing. That pattern holds for the next 6,000 years.

c. 2800 BC
The steppe replacement

Corded Ware / Yamnaya-descended herders sweep into Scandinavia from the Pontic steppe, bringing Indo-European language and horses. The genetic turnover in Scandinavia is among the most complete anywhere in Europe. Modern Norwegian ancestry is overwhelmingly this layer.

793
Lindisfarne

The raid on the Northumbrian monastery is the conventional start of the Viking Age, mostly because a literate monk wrote it down. Norse traders had been moving through the North Sea for generations.

c. 872–930
Harald Fairhair "unifies" Norway

The founding myth: Harald vows not to cut his hair until he rules all Norway, wins at Hafrsfjord, gets a haircut. Historians are sceptical. The sagas were written 300 years later by Icelanders with motives, and real unification took until roughly 1130. Treat it as national mythology with a kernel of truth.

c. 985–1000
Iceland, Greenland, Vinland

Erik the Red settles Greenland; his son Leif reaches North America around 1000. The Norse site at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland is dated by tree rings to exactly AD 1021, 471 years before Columbus. It is the only confirmed pre-Columbian European settlement in the Americas.

1030
Stiklestad and the invention of St. Olav

Olav Haraldsson loses the battle and his life, then wins the argument posthumously: declared a saint within a year, he becomes Norway's eternal king and the engine of Christianization. Nidaros (Trondheim) becomes a major pilgrimage destination.

1066
Stamford Bridge ends the Viking Age

Harald Hardrada is killed invading England. He was Norwegian king, former commander of the Byzantine emperor's Varangian Guard, and the most well-travelled man of his century. Three weeks later Harold Godwinson loses to William at Hastings, and Norway's medieval high-water mark passes.

1262
Peak Norway

The realm covers Iceland, Greenland, the Faroes, Orkney, Shetland, the Hebrides and the Isle of Man. This is the largest Norway ever gets. It is downhill for the next 550 years.

1349
The Black Death, and why Norway lost 460 years

Plague arrives at Bergen on an English ship and kills an estimated 60–65% of the population. Norway's thin aristocracy is effectively wiped out along with its administrative class and written language. The country never recovers institutional independence on its own terms. This single event does more to explain the next four centuries than any king.

1397
Kalmar Union

Denmark, Sweden and Norway unite under one crown. Norway is the weakest leg and stays in after Sweden leaves.

1537–1814
The "400-year night"

Reformation imposed from Copenhagen; Norway is downgraded to a Danish province. Written Norwegian disappears and Danish becomes the language of church, law and letters. Greenland, Iceland and the Faroes are transferred to Danish administration. The phrase "400-year night" is Ibsen's, and it stuck.

1814
The busiest year in Norwegian history

January: Denmark backs Napoleon, loses, and cedes Norway to Sweden at the Treaty of Kiel. April: Norwegians ignore this, convene at Eidsvoll and sign a constitution on 17 May, still the national day and still one of the oldest constitutions in force anywhere. Summer: Sweden invades and wins in about two weeks. November: Norway enters a personal union with Sweden but keeps its constitution and parliament. A defeat that functioned as a founding.

1825–1925
A third of the country leaves

Roughly 800,000 Norwegians emigrate, overwhelmingly to Minnesota, Wisconsin, the Dakotas and Iowa. Per capita, only Ireland sent a larger share of its population to America. About 3.9 million Americans still claim Norwegian ancestry on recent American Community Survey estimates, revised down from the 4.5 million of the 2000 Census. Either figure is comparable to Norway's entire present population.

1905
Independence, by referendum, 99.95% to 0.05%

The union with Sweden dissolves peacefully. The confirming referendum runs 368,208 to 184. Norwegians then vote 79% to be a monarchy rather than a republic and import a Danish prince, who takes the deliberately archaic name Haakon VII. His son Olav and grandson Harald V follow. Harald V, the current king, is the grandson of a man elected to the job.

1911
Amundsen reaches the South Pole

14 December, five weeks ahead of Scott, using dogs and skis while the British hauled sledges by hand. Polar exploration becomes central to Norwegian self-image. See also Nansen (Nobel Peace Prize 1922) and Heyerdahl's Kon-Tiki raft in 1947.

1940–45
Occupation

Germany invades 9 April 1940. In Oslofjord the 19th-century fortress at Oscarsborg sinks the brand-new cruiser Blücher with obsolete guns and torpedoes, delaying the assault long enough for the king, government and gold reserve to escape north and then to London. Vidkun Quisling's name enters English as a common noun. Norwegian saboteurs destroy the heavy-water plant at Vemork in February 1943. Of 772 Jews deported, 34 survived. Retreating Germans burn Finnmark to the ground in 1944. Norway's merchant fleet, around 1,000 ships under Nortraship, is one of the Allies' most valuable assets and takes heavy losses.

1949
NATO founding member

Neutrality died on 9 April 1940 and has never come back. Norway shares a 196 km Arctic land border with Russia.

1969
Ekofisk

Phillips Petroleum, drilling its last contractual well before giving up, hits oil on 23 December. Production starts 1971. Norway already had strong institutions, a competent bureaucracy and an established social contract before the money arrived. That is the standard explanation for why it avoided the resource curse that flattened comparable discoveries elsewhere.

1972 & 1994
Norway says no to Europe. Twice.

EEC membership rejected 53.5–46.5 in 1972; EU membership rejected 52.2–47.8 in 1994. Both times the cities voted yes and the countryside, fishermen and farmers voted no. Norway joined the EEA anyway, so it adopts most EU rules with no vote on them. Norwegians re-litigate the trade-off periodically and then decline to change it.

1996
First deposit into the oil fund

The Government Pension Fund Global receives its first transfer. The stated point was to keep petroleum money out of the domestic economy and smooth spending across generations.

2011
22 July

A far-right terrorist bombs the government quarter in Oslo and shoots dead 69 people, most of them teenagers, at a Labour Party youth camp on Utøya. 77 dead in total. The national response was no emergency powers, no expanded surveillance, and an ordinary criminal trial. That is a deliberate contrast with the American response to 2001, and Norwegians know it.

2022–
Europe's gas station

Russia's invasion of Ukraine turns Norway into the EU's largest single gas supplier, covering over 30% of EU+UK consumption. Windfall revenues are enormous and slightly uncomfortable: Norway is getting rich off a war it is helping the victim survive.
