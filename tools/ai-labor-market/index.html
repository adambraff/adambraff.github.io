Demand is derived

Firms buy insight, and analysts are an input. Insight demand has constant elasticity η. AI multiplies analyst productivity by m on the complement side, and on the substitute side lets managers self-serve a share f of the work.

Q_d = A · (1 − f) · m^(η − 1) · W^(−η)

The exponent η − 1 carries the whole argument: a productivity gain raises labor demand when η > 1 and lowers it when η < 1. This is the Hicks-Marshall rule, and also Jevons applied to analyst hours.

Supply shifts too

AI lowers the skill barrier, so more nontechnical people can do analytics work. That moves supply right by 1 + entry · θ, pushing salaries down and headcount up.

Q_s = B · (1 + e·θ) · W^(ε)

Supply elasticity ε governs how the burden splits: inelastic supply turns a demand shift mostly into higher salaries, elastic supply turns it mostly into more jobs.

Parameter mapping

With AI capability θ and the substitute/complement dial x ∈ [−1, 1]:

m = 1 + 2·θ·max(x, 0) f = 0.75·θ·max(−x, 0)

So full capability with a pure complement triples analyst output, and a pure substitute hands up to 75% of the work to managers with a chatbot. A and B are calibrated so the pre-AI market clears at W = 100, Q = 100.
